This exercise is designed for you to practice requirements elicitation techniques.

Read the _Initial Statement of Requirements_ and _Functional Requirements_ of the case study for practical classes, i.e. the _Mobile Restaurant Booking System_. The documents are available on the student website.

Answer the following questions in the context of this case study.

1. **List the key stakeholders, especially various types of users, in this project and their particular interest in the project.**<br><br>_Note_: It is not just the end users who have an interest in the system, a variety of interest groups have their own requirements and sometimes these are in conflict. Certainly they will have different priorities.

2. **List the fact-finding technique(s) that are suitable for eliciting requirements from each stakeholder (including various types of users) to clarify your understanding of the requirements for the system.**<br><br>_Note_: There are a variety of methods for eliciting information from different stakeholders. It is important to use the most suitable techniques for a given situation and the information to be gleaned.

3. **Write down your understanding of the main goals for the case study system.**<br><br>_Note_: The top down approach to requirements analysis starts with stating the business goals including expected benefits to be won by implementing the proposed system. Then each process within the system that various types of users engage has a specific goal or objective that relates back to the overall goal.

4. **From your understanding of the case study system, examine the list of functional requirements given in the case study, and identify what may have omitted, what is unclear, and what may be wrong.**<br><br>_Note_: At the start of requirement engineering process, even though you are not fully familiar with a system, you are expected to arrive at an initial list of requirements and make some intelligent assumptions. The case study document is such an effort. Thus, it may have problems in clarity, consistency and completeness. Assumptions could be wrong as well.

5. **Based on your understanding of the case study system and the functional requirements defined in the document, list as many as possible the non-functional requirements on the quality of the system.**<br><br>_Note_: At the start of requirements engineering process, you may have to make some assumptions about the system’s usage and operation environment etc. and define the quality requirements accordingly. This definition of quality requirements may not be able to give the full details on all aspects of quality attributes.

6. **Based on the functional requirements defined in the case study document, and your list of quality requirements, design a detailed requirements elicitation plan to find out full details of the requirements on quality of the system.**<br><br>_Note_: You should go back to your answer to question 2 and revise it to produce a detailed design of your requirement elicitation plan aiming at finding out the details of quality requirements. The plan should include: (a) the questionnaires to be distributed to the users, (b) questions to be asked during interviews, (c) key points to be observed in the observation sections, (d) specific types of experts to be invited for workshops, (d) the list of documents to be studied, etc.
