## Aims of the Project Module

- An <span style="color: red">individually supervised</span> project to <span style="color: red">investigate a chosen problem</span> to form an extended study of a topic <span style="color: red">selected from a suitable area</span> of the student’s program of study and that <span style="color: red">involves the solution of a practical problem</span>.
- Examples:
    - Design of a Project Topic Selection System
    - Design of a Breast Cancer Detection System
    - Design of a Product Recommendation System
- Conforms to the appropriate university codes of practice and ethical requirements.

## Project Contents

- Substantial topic related to your BSc course
- Guided by the BCS (the Chartered Institute for IT) requirements (see Handbook)
    - <span style="color: red">ability to apply practical and analytical skills</span>
    - <span style="color: red">innovation and/or creativity</span>
    - <span style="color: red">synthesis of information, ideas, and practices to provide a quality solution with the evaluation of that solution</span>
    - <span style="color: red">the project meets a real need in a wider context</span>
    - <span style="color: red">ability to self-manage a significant piece of work</span>
    - <span style="color: red">critical self-evaluation of the process</span>
- Consideration of Professional Issues: risk, legal, social, ethical, and environmental

## List of Deliverables

- <span style="color: red"><u>You will be assessed on the PROCESS as well as the DELIVERABLES</u></span>
- Must generate deliverables such as: 
    - <span style="color: red">Project proposal</span>: a detailed description of the work to be done.
    - <span style="color: red">Ethics Forms</span>: documents to help you reflect on the ethical implications of your project.
    - <span style="color: red">Progress report</span>: a report of your project progress submitted at the end of semester 1.
    - <span style="color: red">Implemented Project</span>: substantial technical artifact produced according to a suitable methodology.  
    - <span style="color: red">Final Project Report (“Dissertation”)</span>: a detailed report on your specific topic.
    - <span style="color: red">Oral Presentation documents</span>: PowerPoint slides or poster file.
    - <span style="color: red">Weekly reports</span>: a weekly report of your progress. It is designed to enhance with student-supervisor communication.

## Generative AI Usage

- AI can provide learning opportunities if used cautiously, critically and reflectively.
- If you provide clear and accurate prompts to ChatGPT, you are more likely to get responses that are relevant and appropriate for your needs. When used responsibly, ChatGPT can support your learning, your research, and your writing, and ensure that the ideas and arguments presented in your assignments reflect your own understanding of the subject.

Use AI with <span style="color: red">CAUTION</span> (a useful acronym):
- <span style="color: red">C</span>heck your prompts. The information you get out is only as good as the requests
- <span style="color: red">A</span>pproach any information the AI tool produces cautiously (be a critical reader).
- <span style="color: red">U</span>nderstand that Large Language Models (including ChatGPT, Deepseek) are designed only to predict and generate texts.
- <span style="color: red">T</span>ake the time to verify any claims made and check the reliability of any sources.
- <span style="color: red">I</span>dentify any use of AI tools (including large language models such as ChatGPT, Deepseek) in the student declaration form (below). Always declare your use of AI tools and explain how you used them.
- <span style="color: red">O</span>bserve the principles of Good Academic Practice at all times.
- <span style="color: red">N</span>ever submit chunks of text produced by AI as your own work. You may be in breach of the academic conduct regulations.

## Role of Supervisor

- Advise on the scope of the dissertation
- Assess the level of work required
- May suggest literature
- Provide feedback on a technical level and draft versions of parts of written reports
- Advise about managing the timetable of project activities

<span style="color: red"><i>It is not the supervisor’s role to manage the dissertation or the student's time or to motivate the student. The student should manage his or her time, do the work, and write the dissertation.</i></span>

## Supervision: How to Get the Most of Your Supervisor

- Agree on a timetable of weekly meetings and stick to it
- Each meeting should have a focus of your choice
- Prepare for the meeting – record what you worked on, what progress you made, based on the action points from the last meeting, what you want feedback on
- At the end of each meeting, agree on action points
- Keep a record of the meetings: it will help you to present accurate weekly reports to your supervisor. <span style="color: red">(Logbook: Digital records – Use my suggested excel template)</span>
- Submit your weekly report <span style="color: red">(the weekly reports template is on the students’ website)</span> to be signed by the supervisor.
- Be committed to the meetings with your supervisor.

## Do's and Don't

<span style="color: red"><u>DO</u></span>
- Get started straight away
- Make a plan of your time and weekly goals
- Contact your supervisor
- <span style="color: red">Aim to have something designed by Week 10</span>

<span style="color: red"><u>DON’T</u></span>
- Worry if you haven’t got a topic or want to change
- Plan something unachievable in the time, or think that you must be too original
- (Example: Building <span style="color: red">a new generation “Taobao”</span> that is better than the existing one)
